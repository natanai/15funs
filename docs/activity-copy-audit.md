# 15funs: full activity-copy audit

## Purpose and editorial constraints

Audit performed against the existing 73 activities in `data/ideas.csv`. The intended context is **two people on a couch looking for short ways to connect**, sometimes making physical notes, drawings, or objects worth keeping. The shared memory board in the user-provided photo informed that context, but no photo or private content is committed to the repository.

**No activities were added or deleted.** All 73 descriptions were revised for two-person clarity and practical execution. Three titles that implicitly address "me/you" were retitled without changing the activity. Original categories, need-tag strings, energy levels and resource links are unchanged. Every row now contains the **historical ID** it had before editing, so completed-activity history remains associated with the same ideas.

## Editorial standard for each card

1. State who does what using **each**, **one person**, **the other**, **together**, or **switch roles**. Do not let the site impersonate a partner by saying "me" or "our".
2. Give enough detail to start with typical home materials, to understand turns, and to recognize a stopping point. The activity is a suggestion, not a demand to perform perfectly.
3. The displayed duration is an **approximate whole-activity time**. Avoid a second mandatory stopwatch or relying on unseen time signals. The built-in timer is optional.
4. Protect choice and physical comfort in touch, movement, breathing, emotion-sharing, smells and temperature activities.
5. Keep the original creative premise, rather than introduce new games, and leave room for the two people to put their own twist on it.

## Specific changes

- **Doodle swap:** Draw on separate sheets, exchange when both have added a recognizable detail, repeat until the optional 10-minute timer ends. The exchange rule no longer needs multiple separate timer alarms.
- **Charades / 20 Questions:** Define actor or answerer, guesser, turn exchange, and how the site's prompt buttons participate.
- **Tiny nap pact:** The card's five-minute timer is now the only required interval, instead of a three-minute timer embedded inside a ten-minute activity.
- **Back-drawing relay (formerly Draw on my back)** and **Sentence exchange (formerly Read me this):** Names no longer imply the site itself is speaking as one member of the pair.
- **Appreciation cards (formerly What I value in you cards):** Clear two-person naming; still the same exchanged written appreciation activity.
- **Conversation questions:** Five existing prompts no longer ask the person using the app to speak to "me" or "us" as if the website were their partner.
- **Needs tags:** Some source tags (for example `Play`, `Comfort`, `Creativity`, `Touch`) were previously dropped by the app's original fixed-vocabulary normalization. They now remain visible and usable in the Needs filter; no new tags were invented.

## Safety and compatibility checks

- All 73 original historical IDs are preserved exactly and remain unique; descriptions, titles and durations are free to improve without resetting history.
- Original source categories, needs strings, energy strings, links, link labels and row order remain untouched.
- The conversation-question collection remains at 19; the Charades and 20 Questions pools remain at 40 each. Those game prompts were reviewed and retained without additions.
- `node tests/activity-data.mjs` checks CSV shape, catalog size, historical IDs, descriptions, durations, valid links, prompt counts and ambiguous narrator pronouns.
- `node tests/ux-smoke.mjs` checks existing draw/completion logic and the preservation/filtering of existing free-text need tags.

## Remaining real-user verification

A code/content audit cannot replace trying the activities together. During the next session, especially test **Doodle swap**, **Household pictionary**, **Back-drawing relay**, **Charades**, **Two-word check-in** and **Tiny nap pact**, and note any card that still requires inventing a rule. Revisit individual durations based on lived experience rather than treating these estimates as hard deadlines.
